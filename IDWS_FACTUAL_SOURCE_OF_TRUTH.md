# ID Work Studio Factual Source of Truth

**Status:** Canonical factual reference\
**Applies to:** ID Work Studio articles, pages, planning tools,
calculators, FAQs, schema-supporting copy, SEO/GEO content, and future
factual consistency audits\
**Priority:** Safety \> Correctness \> Completeness \> Speed

## 1. Purpose

This file is the canonical factual reference for recurring ID Work
Studio renovation facts.

Its purpose is to prevent different articles, pages, tools, calculators,
FAQs, and future content from gradually publishing conflicting figures
or regulatory claims.

This file does **not** mean every page must use the same number
regardless of context. Different figures may legitimately apply to
different scopes, property types, project complexity, or stages of
planning.

Before changing a recurring factual statement on the website:

1.  identify the exact active page/component/key;
2.  compare its context with this source of truth;
3.  determine whether the difference is a genuine contradiction or a
    legitimate scope difference;
4.  change only verified active content;
5.  do not alter calculator logic without explicit approval.

## 2. Residential Whole-Home Renovation Cost --- 2026

Use the following ranges as the current ID Work Studio residential
whole-home planning framework.

  Property Type         Typical Renovation Budget
  ------------------- ---------------------------
  3-room HDB BTO             S\$40,000--S\$52,000
  4-room HDB BTO             S\$50,000--S\$60,000
  5-room HDB BTO             S\$60,000--S\$75,000
  3-room HDB resale          S\$55,000--S\$68,000
  4-room HDB resale          S\$70,000--S\$81,000
  5-room HDB resale          S\$80,000--S\$98,000

### Interpretation

These are planning ranges, not fixed quotations.

Actual renovation cost can change materially according to:

-   hacking and demolition;
-   existing site condition;
-   carpentry quantity and detailing;
-   wet works;
-   electrical and plumbing work;
-   material selection;
-   design complexity;
-   layout changes;
-   site access and project constraints.

Resale renovation generally carries more uncertainty than BTO renovation
because existing finishes and infrastructure may require removal,
repair, replacement, or upgrading.

### Private homes

Do not automatically apply HDB ranges to condominiums or private
apartments. Private residential cost depends strongly on unit condition,
scope, management requirements, materials, and design complexity.

Where an article provides a private-home range, preserve the context and
verify it separately rather than forcing it into the HDB framework.

## 3. Office Renovation Cost --- Singapore

Use the following as the canonical ID Work Studio office renovation cost
framework.

  Office Fit-Out Level              Planning Range
  -------------------------- ---------------------
  Light / basic SME                 S\$50--S\$80 psf
  Mid-range SME                    S\$80--S\$130 psf
  Standard corporate             S\$100--S\$180 psf
  Premium / Grade A              S\$130--S\$200+ psf
  Complex / high-spec            S\$180--S\$300+ psf

### Preferred short answer

When a concise general answer is required:

> A standard office renovation in Singapore typically costs around
> S\$100--S\$180 psf. Simpler SME fit-outs can start around
> S\$50--S\$80 psf, while premium or technically complex offices can
> exceed S\$180--S\$300 psf.

### Important scope rule

Do not treat every office cost range on the website as contradictory
simply because it differs from S\$100--S\$180 psf.

A different range may be valid when it clearly describes:

-   a light fit-out;
-   a basic fit-out;
-   a premium fit-out;
-   reinstatement rather than renovation;
-   a particular office size;
-   an unusually technical project;
-   a narrower project scope.

Before changing an apparent conflict, verify the exact context and
whether the statement is active/live.

## 4. Commercial Renovation Cost --- Scope Rule

There is no single universal commercial-renovation psf figure that
should be forced across offices, retail, F&B, shophouses, and other
commercial spaces.

Commercial cost is strongly affected by:

-   business use;
-   M&E requirements;
-   fire-safety requirements;
-   kitchen/exhaust requirements;
-   electrical load;
-   plumbing and drainage;
-   landlord/MCST requirements;
-   operating-building restrictions;
-   material and finish level;
-   authority submissions where triggered;
-   reinstatement obligations;
-   project size and complexity.

When discussing commercial renovation generally, explain the scope
drivers instead of presenting an office-specific figure as universally
applicable.

duration.

  Project Scale               Typical Construction / Fit-Out Period
  ------------------------- ---------------------------------------
  Small / straightforward                                3--6 weeks
  Medium                                                6--10 weeks
  Large / complex                                      10--16 weeks

### Preferred general answer

For broad commercial-renovation guidance:

> Many commercial renovation projects in Singapore should be planned
> around approximately 6--12 weeks for the renovation/fit-out itself,
> depending on size, complexity, building restrictions and required
> approvals.

### Approval planning allowance

Where landlord, MCST, building-management, or authority approvals are
relevant, allow approximately:

**1--4 weeks of planning allowance**

This is a planning allowance, not a guaranteed approval duration.

### Important distinction

Do not combine approval time and physical construction time without
explaining the distinction.

Do not force HDB residential renovation timelines into commercial
content.

## 6. HDB Renovation Timeline

The current ID Work Studio planning framework distinguishes BTO and
resale renovation.

Use approximately:

-   **BTO:** 8--12 weeks
-   **Resale HDB:** 10--16 weeks

These are broad project-planning ranges. Actual duration depends on
scope, hacking, wet works, carpentry, approvals, material lead times,
site conditions, variations, and sequencing.

Do not replace these figures with commercial-project timeline ranges.

## 7. HDB Regulatory Principle

HDB requirements are **work-triggered**, not a blanket statement that
every renovation item requires the same approval.

Use careful wording such as:

> Certain HDB renovation works require prior approval or must comply
> with HDB renovation requirements. The exact requirement depends on the
> proposed works.

Relevant examples may include structural/wall alterations, certain
wet-area works, window-related works, and other regulated renovation
items.

### Contractor wording

Where relevant, distinguish between general renovation work and work
that must be carried out by an HDB-registered renovation contractor.

Do not state or imply that HDB approval automatically covers BCA, SCDF,
URA, or other regulatory requirements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. BCA Regulatory Principle

BCA involvement is **trigger-based**.

Do not write that every renovation requires BCA approval.

BCA-related review or professional involvement may become relevant where
the proposed works affect matters such as:

-   structural elements;
-   building works regulated under applicable legislation;
-   alterations requiring professional design/submission;
-   other regulated building-safety matters.

### Safe wording

> Whether BCA involvement is required depends on the nature of the
> proposed works. Structural or regulated building works may require
> professional assessment and the appropriate submission or approval.

Do not use BCA as a generic synonym for "renovation approval."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. SCDF Fire-Safety Principle

SCDF requirements are **scope- and trigger-dependent**.

Do not state that every commercial renovation requires a full SCDF
submission.

Potential triggers can include changes affecting:

-   fire-safety provisions;
-   means of escape;
-   occupant load or use;
-   fire compartments;
-   fire-rated construction;
-   fire-protection systems;
-   exit arrangements;
-   other regulated fire-safety matters.

Depending on the scope, the appropriate route may involve a fire-safety
submission, an exemption, Minor A&A treatment, or another applicable
compliance route.

### Safe wording

> SCDF requirements depend on whether the renovation affects regulated
> fire-safety provisions. The project scope should be reviewed before
> deciding whether a submission, exemption, Minor A&A route, or other
> fire-safety process applies.

Never promise that a project can "skip SCDF" without reviewing the
actual scope.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. URA Planning Principle

URA involvement is **planning-triggered**.

Relevant situations may include:

-   change of use;
-   planning permission;
-   conservation requirements;
-   works affecting conserved properties;
-   other planning-controlled matters.

Do not imply that every interior renovation requires URA approval.

### Safe wording

> URA requirements depend on the approved use of the premises, whether a
> change of use is proposed, and whether planning or conservation
> controls apply.

For shophouses and conserved properties, distinguish interior renovation
from alterations affecting protected or externally visible heritage
elements.

## 11. MCST, Landlord and Building Management

MCST, landlord, and building-management approval are not interchangeable
with statutory authority approval.

Commercial and condominium projects may need to comply with
building-specific requirements such as:

-   renovation applications;
-   deposits;
-   approved working hours;
-   access arrangements;
-   protection requirements;
-   contractor registration;
-   insurance documentation;
-   M&E requirements;
-   submission of drawings;
-   reinstatement obligations.

### Safe wording

> Building-management, landlord or MCST approval may be required in
> addition to any statutory approvals triggered by the renovation scope.

Never imply that MCST/landlord approval replaces SCDF, BCA, URA, HDB, or
other statutory requirements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. NEA Principle

NEA requirements should only be introduced where relevant to the
proposed use or works.

This is particularly important for F&B and other operations involving
environmental-health requirements.

Do not force NEA references into ordinary office or residential
renovation content without a genuine trigger.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. F&B Premises --- Approval and Feasibility Principle

For F&B premises, lease feasibility should be considered before
renovation commitment because operational requirements can materially
affect whether a unit is suitable.

Potential considerations include:

-   approved use;
-   cooking type;
-   exhaust requirements;
-   grease management;
-   drainage;
-   gas/LPG where applicable;
-   electrical load;
-   fire-safety requirements;
-   seating/occupant considerations;
-   landlord/MCST/building-management restrictions;
-   authority requirements triggered by the proposed operation.

Do not imply that the presence of an existing F&B tenant automatically
proves that every proposed F&B concept can operate from the unit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Professional Appointment Principle

Professional appointments must be described as **trigger-based**.

Depending on the project, relevant professionals or specialists may
include:

-   QP;
-   PE;
-   LEW;
-   M&E consultants;
-   fire-safety professionals;
-   other competent persons required for the specific scope.

Do not state that every renovation requires all of these appointments.

### Safe wording

> The need for a QP, PE, LEW, M&E consultant or other professional
> depends on the proposed works and the applicable regulatory
> requirements.

## 15. SS 553 and Technical Standards

Do not present SS 553 or another technical standard as universally
applicable to every renovation.

Reference technical standards only when they are relevant to the system,
design, installation, or compliance issue being discussed.

If a technical standard materially affects a calculator or planning
tool's logic, do not alter the logic solely through an SEO/content
update. Follow the calculator logic-change approval process.

## 16. Cost and Timeline Language

Use language that reflects planning uncertainty.

Preferred terms include:

-   typical;
-   approximately;
-   broad range;
-   planning range;
-   commonly;
-   may;
-   depends on scope;
-   should be verified for the actual project.

Avoid presenting planning ranges as guaranteed quotations or guaranteed
completion periods.

## 17. Factual Conflict Resolution

When two live pages appear to disagree:

### Step 1 --- Identify context

Determine whether they refer to the same:

-   property type;
-   project type;
-   scope;
-   quality tier;
-   time period;
-   stage of project;
-   regulatory trigger.

### Step 2 --- Verify active/live content

A matching number in source code, `i18n.ts`, an old component, or an
unused object is not proof that the live site is publishing it.

### Step 3 --- Classify

Classify the difference as one of:

-   genuine contradiction;
-   legitimate scope difference;
-   inactive/legacy content;
-   calculator-specific logic;
-   insufficient information.

### Step 4 --- Act conservatively

Only genuine contradictions in verified active content should be
corrected.

Do not clean up inactive legacy content as part of an unrelated factual
fix.
